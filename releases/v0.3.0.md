# Papo TUI 0.3.0

Papo TUI 0.3.0 adds message navigation, replies, editing, deletion and reactions,
with dedicated controls for viewing reaction participants. It also expands the
notification center, animates server emojis, adds larger emoji-only messages,
improves media composition and simplifies channel navigation.

## Message navigation and context menus

- Press **F7** to enter message navigation. The selected message receives a
  highlighted border, and ↑/↓ moves between messages. Older and newer history
  pages load as navigation reaches the available edges.
- Use ←/→ to select an attachment within the message. The border indicates the
  selected media number; Enter opens that media. Closing the viewer returns to
  the selected message.
- Press **m** to open a context menu next to the selected message, or right-click
  any visible message to open its menu directly. The keyboard menu follows the
  selected message when scrolling or resizing the terminal.
- Context actions include **Responder** (Reply), **Adicionar reação** (Add
  reaction), **Ver quem reagiu** (See who reacted), **Ir para mensagem original**
  (Go to original message), editing your own messages and deletion.
- Press **m** again or Esc to close the menu. Esc or F7 leaves message navigation
  and returns to the previous focus. Pasting into an open menu does not edit or
  send the conversation draft.

## Replies and original-message previews

Choose **Responder**, or press **r** during message navigation, to focus the
editor with a reply target. The reply strip identifies the original message,
which remains highlighted while you compose your answer.

Replies display a gray **↰ Author: excerpt** above their content. Previews
resolve user and webhook author names, display mentions as readable names and
can identify an attachment when the original has no text. Click the preview or
choose **Ir para mensagem original** to find and highlight the original.

Originals outside loaded history are looked up automatically within the active
conversation, without moving your reading position merely to fill a preview.
Loading, failed lookups and unavailable originals have distinct states; a failed
request is not treated as proof that the original was deleted. Failed jumps can
be retried explicitly with Ctrl+R.

Reply targets belong to each conversation's draft during the current run. Manual
send retries retain the original target captured for that send, even if you have
already started a reply to another message. A confirmed send clears its reply
target. Esc in the editor or **[x]** cancels a reply while preserving your text;
Esc keeps the editor focused so you can continue composing an ordinary message.

## Message editing

Choose **Editar mensagem** (Edit message) on your own message. Alternatively,
press ↑ in the focused, empty editor to edit your newest own message in loaded
history. This shortcut does not search unloaded pages, and ↑ keeps its normal
cursor behavior when the editor contains text.

The editor shows **Editando mensagem** (Editing message). The original scrolls
into view and stays highlighted with an **✎ Editando** border while editing.
Enter saves the new text; Esc or **[x]** cancels. Shift+Enter continues to insert
a new line. Editing preserves attachments and custom embeds, and your send draft
and reply target return when editing ends.

Empty edits are refused. Messages longer than the editor's 2,048-character limit
cannot be loaded for editing. Failed edits keep the edit buffer for an explicit
retry. Typing while an edit is pending preserves the newer text in editing mode;
pressing Enter again does not submit a duplicate pending edit.

Changing conversation, server, account or session closes editing. A remotely
deleted or unavailable message also closes its edit state instead of turning the
edit into a new outgoing message. Already submitted requests may still finish,
but their results are kept separate from the next conversation.

## Message deletion

**Excluir mensagem** (Delete message) opens a confirmation with **Cancelar**
(Cancel) selected initially. The destructive button uses white text on red only
while focused. Confirm with Enter or the mouse; Esc cancels.

The server checks whether you own the message or have the applicable deletion
permission. Failed deletions leave the message visible and are not replayed
automatically. Successful local deletions and remote deletion events update the
history and close an edit of that message when necessary.

## Reactions and participant lists

Reactions appear below message text as emoji/count buttons and wrap onto another
row when needed. Click a button to add your reaction; click it again to remove
it. Your own reactions are highlighted. **Adicionar reação**, or **e** during
message navigation, opens a picker for Unicode and server emojis without
inserting anything into your draft or changing its reply target.

Counts incorporate real-time reaction updates without double-counting the local
confirmation. Empty reaction groups disappear when their count reaches zero.
Failures preserve the displayed state for an explicit retry; uncertain outcomes
are reconciled with history before another mutation. Rate-limit responses do
not cause automatic reaction resubmission.

**Ver quem reagiu**, or **Shift+E** during message navigation, opens a two-column
participant panel:

- The left column shows rendered Unicode/server emojis and their message totals.
- The right column shows people who reacted with the selected emoji.
- ↑/↓, PageUp/PageDown and the mouse wheel scroll each column independently.
  Enter or → moves from the emoji list to its participants; ← returns.
- Selecting an emoji filters its participants and loads missing pages when
  necessary. Completed groups do not trigger prefetch of unrelated groups.
- Participant pagination preserves group selection. Failures wait for Ctrl+R,
  and reconnect recovery closes stale participant panels.

Closing the panel or removing a reaction retires its old image placements,
including Unicode and custom emojis that share image data with the history.

## Notification search, filters and ordering

The F9 notification center now supports local text search across loaded message
previews, displayed mentions, author names and conversation names. Search and
filtering do not mark notifications read.

Choose **Todos** (All), **Canais** (Channels) or **DMs** to filter the results.
Clicking a category or using ←/→ changes that category without opening a selector
or unexpectedly moving focus into a name field. To select a specific channel or
DM, press Enter on the filter or use the separate **[Nome]** (Name) button,
positioned immediately beside the filter label.

The conversation selector has its own **Nome** field, keyboard navigation and
mouse selection. Search channel names or a DM recipient's name/@username.
**Todos os canais** (All channels) and **Todas as DMs** (All DMs) restore the
whole category. Canceling the selector preserves the notification search text.

**Data** (Date) switches between newest and oldest first, with precise timestamp
ordering. Notifications without a known date follow dated results. Arrow keys
move between search, filter/date controls and results; inside search, ←/→ moves
the text cursor instead of changing the filter.

Search, filters and ordering cover loaded notifications. **Mais antigas** (Older)
loads another page, while Ctrl+R refreshes or retries a failed request.

## Notification destinations and author attribution

**Abrir conversa** (Open conversation) preserves both the conversation and the
message identified by the notification. It can switch channels, reopen a closed
DM and locate an older message before selecting the target.

The destination receives a highlighted border and stays centered while media,
reply previews or changed content above it finish loading, when enough
surrounding content is available. Resizing retains this positioning. Scrolling
or manually choosing another message releases the centering.

Missing targets produce an explicit notice instead of selecting a different
message. Failed lookups retain the target for an explicit retry. Failed DM-open
operations do not repeatedly reopen the conversation in the background.

Notification author names can be recovered from available message metadata,
including a null author ID on webhook messages. Cached webhook names survive
conversation switches and partial events that omit their attribution. If an
active-conversation notification lacks its author, a bounded lookup can recover
the original message without moving your reading position.

For inactive conversations, **Autor ainda não carregado** (Author not loaded yet)
remains explicit until the conversation is opened. The client does not fetch
inactive histories just to fill author names, because those history reads also
advance remote read state.

## Unread indicators and mention counters

Server tabs and conversation rows now distinguish ordinary unread activity from
known mentions of you or **@everyone**. Mention metadata from available messages
can supplement truncated notification previews without mixing conversations or
instances that happen to use the same message UUID.

Graphical badges use vertically centered red circles with white counts; larger
counts use wider rounded badges. Server badges sit inside the tab delimiters,
with spacing that accounts for long names. Chafa, Light and Ultralight use plain
red server counters, while text conversation indicators use circled digits or
parentheses. A **+** indicates that more notification pages are available.

Opening a notification clears its mention indicator after confirmation. The
conversation's unread-message indicator follows conversation read state
separately. A server can also show unread activity when a notification omits
message metadata. Counters describe available previews and message metadata,
not a guaranteed server-wide unread total.

## Animated server emojis and larger emoji messages

Normal mode now animates server emojis in message bodies, reaction buttons,
participant panels, the editor and emoji selectors. GIF and animated PNG are
supported; animated WebP requires FFmpeg's **webp_anim** decoder.

Light displays the first frame of server emojis, while an attached animation
with identical file contents can still animate independently. Static emoji
variants no longer freeze those shared attachments. Ultralight retains text
representations. Switching modes updates the relevant rendered placements.

The client accepts custom emoji assets up to **2 MiB** and **2048-pixel**
dimensions, with a response budget that accommodates full 25-item base64 catalog
pages. These are client loading bounds; backend emoji upload limits still apply.

Messages containing only rendered emojis now use **48×48** previews and taller
rows. They wrap to fit the conversation, reflow on resize and crop correctly at
history edges. Reading anchors remain attached to the same content while
scrolling or loading older pages. Mixed text and reaction buttons retain inline
sizes. Light keeps enlarged server emojis static and native Unicode rendering;
Ultralight retains its text layout.

## Video covers and larger media downloads

Visible video attachments in Normal mode download in the background to generate
static first-frame covers, including video containers with audio. Covers do not
play or produce sound in the history. Opening the player reuses the same cached
file instead of starting an additional download while it remains available.
Light and Ultralight skip video downloads, covers and playback.

Client download limits increase as follows:

| Media | Previous limit | 0.3.0 limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attached image | 16 MiB | 32 MiB |
| Avatar | 2 MiB | 16 MiB |
| Video | 128 MiB | 256 MiB |

The cached-file budget grows to accommodate the new video limit. Covers, image
variants and playback reuse the existing shared media cache. Oversized files
show **Mídia excede o limite de tamanho.** (Media exceeds the size limit).
These limits apply to client downloads, not the instance's upload rules.

## History rendering behind floating panels

Image previews, avatars, inline emojis and video covers remain visible in the
uncovered parts of the history while a floating panel is open. This applies to
message context menus, mention/emoji selectors, notification panels and reaction
dialogs, including their opening, updates, resizing and closure.

Only covered image regions are hidden. Cropped variants reuse the existing
bounded cache and media helper, keeping decoding and rendering work outside the
TUI's event loop. The composition works with Kitty, Sixel and iTerm2 protocols,
and with Chafa character rendering in fallback and Light modes. Ultralight keeps
message actions available without rendering media.

When a reaction's size or layout changes, Kitty now removes its old image
placement while retaining other occurrences that share the same image data.
This fixes stale reaction images after count changes and panel closure.
Navigation icons also survive partial redraws, and notification badge graphics
use the shared helper/cache rather than a separate rendering path.

## Channel navigation and input shortcuts

Opening a server automatically opens its first channel in list order, skipping
category separators. Initial loading preserves navigation focus and sidebar
visibility, including in narrow terminals. Empty lists and category-only lists
do not trigger history requests.

Categories remain visible but cannot be selected by mouse or keyboard. Arrow
keys, Home/End and PageUp/PageDown skip them. Channel labels move farther left,
with color highlighting instead of leading selection markers. Browsing the list
does not replace the active conversation or read another channel's history;
confirming the current channel preserves its pending read, draft and position.

**Ctrl+Backspace** deletes the word before the cursor in terminals that report
the modified key. The decoder accepts CSI-u and modifyOtherKeys forms, including
CapsLock/NumLock variants. Legacy backspace continues to delete one character.

On a voice channel in the list, **Shift+Enter** opens its message history;
**F4** remains available as an alternative. The shortcut supports regular and
keypad Enter encodings with lock modifiers. In the editor, Shift+Enter continues
to insert a new line; Ctrl+N is the newline fallback. Voice calls are not
available yet.

## Request handling, integration and compatibility

New message operations use the existing authenticated dispatcher and global
request budget. Replies, pending edits and reactions are tied to their instance,
conversation and session context, so a late result cannot mutate another
conversation's draft or reaction state. Operations already sent can finish
after a switch without being applied to the newly active conversation.

Edits, deletions and reactions do not automatically repeat on failure or 429.
Uncertain outcomes reconcile with the active history before an explicit retry.
Reply/original-message lookups have bounded caches and are canceled on context
changes. These features use the existing service API and do not require new
backend endpoints.

Automated coverage expands to message navigation, replies, reaction participants,
editing/deletion, notification attribution and destinations, animation formats,
shared-image placement, enlarged emojis, panel clipping and input protocols.
Reply/reaction contract fixtures and WebSocket fixture coverage are extended.
The development integration probe can exercise these message operations against
an isolated conversation, keeping execution reports under **build/**.
